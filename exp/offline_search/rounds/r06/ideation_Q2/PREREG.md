# Q2 P3 v2 preregistration: how much policy inference to buy

Protocol version: Q2-P3-1, 2026-09-28. This protocol is written before reading pilot outcomes. Only the supplied `tables_v2_smoke8b` product will be opened for implementation testing; its outcomes are plumbing data and will not be interpreted. No pilot directory is inspected or polled in this work. This document supersedes the old Q2 test plan for analysis of P3; the historical report remains a separate record.

## 1. Design, population, and what is identifiable

Design sources: `../p3_profiling/HANDBACK.md`, section “One pilot and continuation campaign”; `SCHEMA_V2.md`; `campaign_v2.py`; `read_v2.py`; `pilot_stats.py`. Guard source: `../ideation_G/REPORT.md`, §§2–3. The campaign has eight cells, nine cohorts, three seed blocks, and 20 task/init pairs per pilot cohort/block. Fixed cohorts are a **complete matched block**, with cohort probability 1; they are not randomized with propensity 1/9. Pair on cell, task, original init, and block, check the actual environment seed, and cluster repeated blocks of the same task/init together. Never count anchors as independent terminal outcomes.

Primary population: equal task weight, equal weight to distinct observed inits within task, and equal weight to the available seed blocks within each init. This explicitly prevents heavily repeated inits in the cumulative campaign from receiving extra population weight. Also report raw episode counts and the campaign's episode-weighted planning precision. This is inference over the represented init distribution, not unseen scenes or independently built libraries. Init IDs are fixed in the manifests: exchangeable-init intervals are model-based, not an exact randomized-init sampling guarantee. Cross-library comparisons would require the same task/init/block intersection and remain a separate secondary analysis, not an implemented result here; the library itself was not randomized.

The fixed doses are A=0, dose125=1/8, dose25=1/4, dose50=1/2, P10=1 at **vision anchors**, with the existing ten-control commitment. B is the deployed guard policy. Factorial and window are different intervention packages and are displayed separately, never fitted as ordinary dose points. `dose_mix` chooses one episode dose from the same five values uniformly ex ante; use its logged episode assignment probability (expected 1/5), not anchor propensity or a guessed cohort probability.

P10 is the contemporaneous pure-policy-at-ten-control-commitment reference. It is not a newly measured native policy with a different observation/commitment cadence. A claim about native pure-inference SR additionally needs a paired native-cadence reference. Historical pure-inference SR is not substituted for this paired endpoint.

Calibration split is exactly `init % 5 == 0`, across every block. Reserved validation is `init % 5 != 0`. Output all/calibration/validation separately. The all-pilot curves are descriptive; fitting or choosing a method from them does not make them validation. Pilot validation has only one distinct init per task, so task-stratified uncertainty is unavailable. No method will be promoted on this pilot. Library-only allocations defined below are frozen without looking at either outcome split.

## 2. Estimands and accounting

For each accepted episode, let Y be terminal success, N be decision requests, V be vision anchors, M be **served policy MISS calls**, and C be actual active controls, excluding reset/warmup. Check the episode totals against decision and anchor tables, accepted attempt identity, actual controls, executed source, logged propensities, and environment seed. Profiling shadows and K4 resamples are not M. Failed completed episodes remain in the denominator; an unaccepted attempt is not another trial. Missing assigned episodes or asymmetric arm support invalidate complete-cell conclusions and are reported, not silently dropped.

Historical owner accounting, fixed for comparability with the previous Q2 report and the v2 reader, is c1=.152 for pi05 and .148 for GR00T. These are accounting constants, not measurements of this profiler's latency. On another robot, obtain c1 by timing the production vision stage and full policy on recorded library inputs under the intended deployment backend; store its provenance. Do not use full-shadow profiler wall time as deployment IR.

Primary SR is the task/init/block standardized mean of Y. Primary owner-IR is the ratio of standardized means `[c1 V + (1-c1) M] / [C/5]`; this prices expected deployment compute per active five-control interval. Report also M/V (anchor call fraction), m=M/N (request MISS share), m5=M/(C/5), vision share, total M per episode, and request-denominated IR. Ratios are ratios of standardized totals, not the unweighted mean of episode ratios. The unit 5 is the current request/control protocol, read and checked against telemetry; transfer must use the target protocol's R. P10 need not have request m=1: a policy tail is not another policy call.

Primary contrasts, per cell and per task:

1. Each of A, dose125, dose25, dose50, B versus P10: paired SR loss, owner-IR saving, and change in M.
2. A and each intermediate fixed dose versus B: whether spending a similar or smaller budget can preserve B's SR.
3. Adjacent assigned doses: delta SR, delta m, delta owner-IR, and their ratios. A slope is a **difference between whole policies**, not an individual call effect. Report marginal SR per unit m and IR only when the corresponding two-sided 95% denominator interval excludes zero on the positive side; otherwise mark it unresolved. Do not enforce monotonic SR or concavity. Diminishing returns require decreasing adjacent slopes supported by their joint uncertainty; otherwise the conclusion is unresolved. Raw differences and negative slopes remain visible.
4. Fraction of the A-to-P10 SR gap closed: `(SR_d-SR_A)/(SR_P10-SR_A)`, only if the paired 95% denominator interval is positive. If A matches/exceeds P10 or the gap is unresolved, no fraction is inferred. Do not clip to [0,1].
5. Library allocation policies below versus P10, B, and their uniform-allocation counterpart. These are expected **episode-lottery policies** with identified mixtures of observed fixed-dose controllers, not off-policy estimates of an adaptive gate or of the old deterministic rotor.

Uncertainty: resample init clusters **within task**, sharing every resample across all cohorts, seed blocks, endpoints, and mixture policies. Use 4,000 draws, NumPy PCG64 seed 26092802, percentile 95% intervals for descriptive curves and paired differences. Retain all seed blocks of a selected init. The point estimand averages blocks first. Fewer than two inits in any represented task makes this population variance unestimable: show point estimates, but null intervals and an explicit reason. Zero-variance/degenerate bootstrap intervals never establish equivalence. Per-task estimates use that task's init clusters only. For screening/certification, use the paired task-stratified cluster-mean influence-function standard error, Student-t with degrees of freedom `sum_t(K_t-1)`, and the simultaneous rule below; this is an approximate independent-init sampling model, not an exact randomization guarantee. Report the paired ICC with task fixed effects using the profiler's `pilot_stats.icc`, retaining negative estimates. Do not convert an estimated negative ICC into fictitious extra samples.

`dose_mix` is a separate randomized replication. For each assigned dose d, compute episode Horvitz–Thompson scores `1[D=d] Z / e_d` for Y, V, M, N, C/5 over **all** dose_mix episodes, using logged `e_d`. Standardize and cluster as above. Report dose support, assigned clusters, and Kish weight ESS; ESS is not an independent sample count. Zero observed support is unresolved, not SR=0. A Hájek outcome sensitivity is secondary. Compare randomized and matched fixed-dose estimates without pooling by default. No sequential importance-product OPE, no regression of success on realized M, and no causal stratification on final progress, total M, or termination length.

## 3. Portable budget algorithm and the single knob

**The owner-facing knob is target deployment owner-IR rho (ρ), not guard level alpha (α).** The SR acceptance margin below is the frozen scientific interpretation of “approximately pure inference”; it is not another online tuning knob. A rho setting is a cost target, not a promise of its SR. If safety cannot be established, report that uncertainty rather than assert that a library proxy certifies it.

Guard report G measures successful-library false alarms ranging from 0 to 38% under current constants and proposes a common alpha. That is a useful internal calibration coordinate, but alpha is neither policy-call share nor owner-IR. Sparse per-task calibration has coarse p-values; P3 does not introduce G's pooled fallback or a common-alpha controller. Its frozen metric LOEO tables are not a newly certified conformal deployment distribution. Consequently the pilot cannot estimate SR of a novel alpha gate by replaying alarms. We do not expose both alpha and rho to the owner.

Exact frozen baseline algorithm (no fitted LIBERO task constants):

1. Read the action/observation adapter and library manifest: task/context key, valid state/action dimensions, request length R, chunk length H, policy/vision cost fraction c1, and task traffic weights (equal task weights if none supplied). Set commitment b=min(2,floor(H/R)); reject H<R. The integer 2 is the declared algorithm default shared across robots, not tuned per task. Library episodes supply decision lengths L; compute per-task `h_t=mean(L)` and `a_t=mean(ceil(L/b))`.
2. Accept Q1's **frozen, nonnegative, higher-is-worse risk** q_t plus library/fit provenance. It must use library data and, if supplied, calibration trajectories only; no validation outcomes. The script's `--quality` CSV interface is `cell,task,risk,mean_decisions,mean_anchors,provenance`. Without a Q1 artifact, use the earlier Q2 measured `state_loeo_distance` from `library_quality.json` with its `mean_decisions` and `mean_anchors_b2`: valid-state standardized RMS distance to the nearest row in another source episode, averaged first by source episode then by task. This is a deliberately weak fallback, not a trusted SR surrogate. Reject missing/negative risks or invalid lengths. Replace q=0 by machine epsilon times the largest positive risk; if all q=0 use uniform weights. Normalize w_t=q_t/mean(q) (traffic weighted when traffic weights are supplied).
3. Let p_t=min(1,lambda*w_t). Solve for lambda by 80 bisection iterations so that `sum_t pi_t*a_t*[c1+(1-c1)*p_t] / sum_t pi_t*h_t = rho`. Clamp rho below the library A floor or above the every-anchor ceiling to the corresponding endpoint and report infeasibility. This budgets total compute across tasks, not equal absolute MISS counts: predicted per-task calls are a_t p_t. A long task consumes more of the budget. Uniform allocation sets every w_t=1 and solves the same equation.
4. To obtain a policy identifiable from this pilot, at episode start choose between the two adjacent fixed doses in D={0,1/8,1/4,1/2,1} with probabilities giving expected dose p_t; at a knot choose that dose with probability one. Keep the chosen Bernoulli anchor-dose controller for the entire episode, with A retrieval and the frozen commitment. Do not switch doses based on observed episode difficulty. Store the episode lottery seed and propensity. This differs from executing a Bernoulli(p_t) policy on every episode; the latter has an unmeasured intermediate-dose trajectory distribution.
5. Estimate the lottery's SR, total calls, and IR by applying its fixed per-task mixture weights to each complete matched task/init/block of the five fixed cohorts, then standardizing. Recompute ratios from mixed numerators/denominators. This is a model-based interpolation only in the sense of known randomization over **whole observed policies**; no linearity of within-episode SR in p is assumed. Include cluster intervals and observed-minus-library-predicted IR. The predictive IR from library lengths can be wrong because interventions change duration; do not silently retune on validation.
6. Predeclare rho grid {.10,.15,.20,.25}, each with risk and uniform allocation. These are analysis checkpoints, not extra owner knobs. If the library predicts a clamped endpoint, retain that fact. On a new benchmark, the same algorithm can generate any feasible rho; a few recorded trajectories calibrate cost/length drift but cannot certify near-pure SR. A later alpha gate may spend this budget by ranking calibrated guard urgency on recorded calibration trajectories. Its thresholds can be chosen by an empirical quantile matching the predicted task call budget, with randomized boundary ties, but its closed-loop SR requires a new paired arm. It is not an evaluated method in this pilot.

Q1 validation: publish per-task A-to-P10 gaps joined to the pre-frozen risks; report within-cell Spearman associations as exploratory and compare risk versus uniform policies directly on held-out inits. No selection among proxy variants using validation SR. Across-cell transfer is unresolved with two library sizes and no independent bank replicates. If risk allocation has no benefit, keep the uniform allocator; do not retrofit task lookup tables from these 20 inits. `ideation_Q1/PREREG.md` distinguishes state disagreement from SR prediction and does not fit a universal eight-bank success map; this protocol agrees. Q1's provisional 5 pp screening margin is not substituted for Q2's 2 pp acceptance margin. Q1 primarily reports request IR; both groups can reconcile using this script's `IR_request` and actual-controls `IR` columns. An imported Q1 CSV must explicitly map its bank tags to the actual frozen P3 library/metric provenance; do not infer a bank from a misleading historical filename. `quality_adapter_example.csv` demonstrates the interface using the already measured Q2 fallback. Changing to a Q1 score selected using pilot validation outcomes requires new held-out data and a versioned protocol. Nonuniform traffic also requires a newly declared population estimand; the pilot CLI deliberately fixes equal task traffic.

## 4. Frozen method-choice rule

Safety margin epsilon=.02 absolute SR, chosen to operationalize the owner's near-pure objective, not estimated from pilot. There are 13 candidate controllers per cell: A, three intermediate doses, B, and eight lottery policies (four rho values × risk/uniform). P10 is the reference. The primary simultaneous selection family contains 13 candidate-vs-P10 SR tests, 12 non-B candidate-vs-B SR tests, and 12 non-B candidate-vs-B IR tests per cell: 37 × 8 = 296 one-sided tests. Use alpha_family=.05 and Bonferroni `.05/296`; report unadjusted descriptive intervals too. Additional curve, slope, dose_mix, and task-level analyses are exploratory and cannot trigger deployment selection.

Before selecting a candidate require the exact full matched cohort/task/init/block support, validated accounting, no unresolved assignment violations, at least two held-out init clusters in every task, and nondegenerate uncertainty for each required SR contrast. The pilot validation split fails the init condition by design. Approximate t intervals are assumptions-based evidence, not formal finite-sample SR guarantees.

Among candidates whose adjusted lower bound on SR(candidate)−SR(P10) exceeds −.02, consider B eligible on that condition alone. For any other candidate additionally require lower SR(candidate)−SR(B)>−.02 and upper IR(candidate)−IR(B)<0. Select the lowest measured owner-IR among eligible candidates; break exact ties by uniform before risk, then lower rho, then cohort name. If none qualifies, retain P10 as the measured SR reference and report **inconclusive**; this is an analysis recommendation, not an instruction to change running experiments. If B is absent, produce the available comparisons but no complete recommendation.

A risk allocator is preferred to its same-rho uniform counterpart only if its held-out point SR is no lower and its measured IR is no higher; otherwise retain uniform at that rho for the recommendation. This additional conservative preference cannot rescue a failed P10/B test. The paired risk-minus-uniform SR and IR intervals must be shown; point dominance is a simplicity tie-break, not proof of a general allocation advantage. Evidence that extra doses harm SR rejects monotone gap-closure assumptions. A nonpositive A-to-P10 gap makes “fraction recovered” undefined and makes A a candidate for direct validation.

## 5. What the pilot/full campaign can resolve; continuation request

From HANDBACK (planning assumptions, not measured ICC): paired difference variance .20, repeated-init correlation .3 gives pilot n_eff=37.5 per cohort/cell, 95% half-width 14.3 pp, 80% MDE 20.5 pp. Full 500 episodes/cohort/cell has 250 distinct init clusters; episode-weighted n_eff=352.1, half-width 4.67 pp, MDE 6.68 pp. The pilot's held-out subset is smaller still. Our equal-init weighting changes cumulative effective size; the script reports that calculation separately. Each pilot task has only two distinct inits; anchors, shadow calls, and K4 samples do not create additional task success trials.

If inconclusive, retain the existing nine cohorts and request the already specified disjoint continuation: 440 more episodes per cohort/cell, 3,960 per cell, 31,680 across eight cells. For Q2, the six fixed/reference cohorts A/dose125/dose25/dose50/P10/B are indispensable (21,120 continuation episodes across eight cells); dose_mix is a randomized replication (3,520 more); factorial/window continue for the shared profiler's other questions. Same frozen library fits, matching manifests, and seed blocks; no rerun of pilot slots. Their main deliverable is a better dose curve and ICC, not a promised 2 pp certificate.

To validate implementation, the optional additional plan has at most eight **configurations**: risk episode lottery versus uniform lottery at rho=.15, in one sparse and one dense cell per policy (four paired comparisons = eight configurations). This is a fixed cost-calibration checkpoint, not a promise that .15 attains the SR target. For each policy choose the largest mean fallback library risk among 50-episode cells and the smallest among 500-episode cells; break ties lexicographically. `additional_arm_cells.json` records the computed choices: pi05_sp_50, pi05_l10_500, groot_sp_50, groot_sp_500. Each arm uses A retrieval, the frozen library/fit, ten-control commitment, episode-start lottery weights from the rho=.15 rows of `budget_library_predictions.csv`, and independent Bernoulli source coins at the chosen fixed anchor dose for the rest of that episode. Generate the episode lottery uniform variate from SHA256 of `(library manifest digest, task key, original init, seed block, "Q2-episode-dose-v1")`; serialize that tuple as a UTF-8 JSON array with separators `(",",":")` and no extra whitespace, use the first 64 bits of the digest in big-endian order as an unsigned integer divided by 2^64 and inverse-CDF sample the recorded mixture. Pair the two allocators and existing B/P10 on exactly the same task/init/environment-seed manifests. Policy-noise seeding follows the P3 profile seed contract. Use the cumulative manifest's 500 episodes per configuration as an implementation check (4,000 episodes total), not a 2 pp powered certificate. Any enlargement follows the precision recipe below. These are exact proposed policies, not runnable profiler arm specs: the coordinator would need an episode-dose lottery adapter; none is built or launched here.

Predicted outcome is explicitly a hypothesis: the two allocations have equal library-predicted IR=.15 in all four chosen cells; their realized costs and SR may differ because they visit different states and terminate at different times. There is no defensible numerical prior for a positive risk-allocation SR advantage. The decisive comparison is risk versus uniform at the same nominal budget, plus both versus B/P10. No new configuration is needed to estimate the expected lottery policy from complete fixed-dose blocks; run these only to check actual lottery implementation and unprofiled runtime. If a new alpha/guard placement policy is proposed, it replaces the risk-lottery configuration and needs a new preregistration; it cannot inherit the lottery's SR estimate. Native-cadence pure-inference validation, if required, must likewise be explicitly substituted/paired rather than called P10 by assumption.

Sample-size recipe: on calibration only, estimate paired init-cluster variance for candidate−P10 and candidate−B (and ICC sensitivity). For desired unadjusted half-width h use ceil(1.96²*sigma_D²/h²) effective episodes; for 80% power MDE delta use ceil((1.959964+.841621)²*sigma_D²/delta²). For noninferiority margin .02 at zero true loss use ceil((z_(1-alpha)+.841621)²*sigma_D²/.02²), with the actual selection-family alpha if selecting multiple candidates. Convert to new init clusters using repeat weights; count the validation fraction, paired arm multiplicity, and stock-init ceiling. Compute and publish both sigma_D²=.20 planning sensitivity and measured values without claiming that either is guaranteed. Prefer new independent inits over repeating a few old ones when ICC is positive. Library/robot transfer needs independent libraries and another robot/benchmark; increasing seed repeats in these eight cells cannot establish it.

The computed planning artifact `power_preregistered.json` gives full-campaign equal-init n_eff=336.323 at assumed ICC=.3: 95% half-width 4.780 pp and 80% MDE 6.832 pp on **all** episodes, before validation splitting and selection multiplicity. Under variance .20, unadjusted ±2 pp needs 1,921 effective paired units; 80%-power 2 pp noninferiority at true zero loss needs 3,092 for one one-sided .05 test or 9,795 under the frozen 296-test family. These are effective units, not additional anchors or a promise about observed variance. Per-task 2 pp certification would require these counts per task under the same variance assumption, far beyond two pilot inits or the stock pool. Report realized per-task counts and uncertainty; do not fit 80 precise task budgets from this pilot.

## 7. Remaining limits and what would change the recommendation

1. **Precise near-pure SR and per-task call benefit remain missing.** The planned profiler fixes observational richness but not the number of independent initializations. A sufficiently powered reserved-init comparison could replace “inconclusive” with a selected budget or show that even B/intermediate doses fail the owner's SR target. Shadow disagreement and resamples cannot supply those terminal counterfactuals.
2. **Native-cadence pure inference and production timing remain missing.** P10 identifies the ten-control policy baseline. If the owner's pure reference has another cadence, obtain paired native-controller outcomes; calibrate stage costs with shadow profiling disabled on recorded trajectories. These can change both the SR reference and the optimal deployment budget. No profiler wall-clock speedup is inferred here.
3. **Absolute quality-to-SR transfer remains missing.** Eight cells with two sizes do not provide independent library-build or robot replicates. Q1 risk could correlate with disagreement while allocating calls to tasks with little positive causal benefit. A failed held-out risk-versus-uniform comparison retains uniform allocation. New benchmark/library builds can change this conclusion; the risk formula and normalization must be frozen before those outcomes.
4. **Adaptive placement at alpha is not evaluated.** The existing cohorts identify fixed Bernoulli dose policies, episode lotteries, and factorial/window packages. A guard level, hard episode cap, deterministic rotor, or mid-episode budget adaptation changes the trajectory law. It needs its own paired controller evaluation or well-supported branch/continuation design. Restore-uncertified snapshots and naive replay do not make those SRs observable. A beneficial calibrated alpha gate could later replace uniform placement while rho remains the cost knob.
5. **Library-predicted IR can drift.** A finite expected-rho allocation is not a hard per-episode cap. If realized durations/anchor schedules differ from library estimates, the script reports the forecast error. Recalibration can use a few designated recorded calibration trajectories, but its uncertainty and the new weights need a versioned holdout evaluation. A budget that only works after outcome-specific task tuning fails the portability objective.

The final source/protocol/library hashes are recorded in `PREREG_LOCK.json`. Smoke audit hashes identify the protocol as it stood at execution, before this software-evidence appendix was finalized. No pilot outcomes were opened during either registration or testing.
