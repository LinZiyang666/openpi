# Step 1a Cache Builder Failure Analysis

Data source: `data/deviation_experiment/cache_eval_results.json`.

Scope: only the three cache-builder configs are included:

- `clip_w7_d4`
- `max_pool_w3_d5`
- `spatial16_w8_d4`

All `inference_*` rows are intentionally excluded. Each builder has 500 rows:
10 `libero_spatial` tasks x 50 default `.pruned_init` states. A unit below is
identified by `(task_id, init_state_idx)`.

## Summary

Overall success rates are close, but the failures are not interchangeable:

| builder | success | failure |
|---|---:|---:|
| `clip_w7_d4` | 337/500 (67.4%) | 163 |
| `max_pool_w3_d5` | 348/500 (69.6%) | 152 |
| `spatial16_w8_d4` | 346/500 (69.2%) | 154 |

Across the 500 shared init units:

| category | units |
|---|---:|
| all 3 pass | 223 |
| exactly 1 builder fails | 136 |
| exactly 2 builders fail | 90 |
| all 3 builders fail | 51 |

Interpretation:

- The 51 all-fail units are likely genuinely hard init states for this policy/cache setting.
- The 90 exact-two failures show builder-specific complementarity: one builder can recover a nontrivial number of cases where the other two fail.
- The 136 exact-one failures are builder-specific weaknesses rather than task-inherent failures.

Core conclusion:

The interesting result is that similar aggregate success rates do not imply
equivalent keybuilders. The three builders land around the same total success
rate, but they do so by succeeding and failing on different task/init slices.
This suggests two things at once:

- Some tasks or init states are broadly hard for this cache-builder family:
  51 init units fail under all three builders.
- Other failures are builder-specific: 136 init units fail under exactly one
  builder, and 90 fail under exactly two builders. This is evidence that the
  keybuilders have different task/init preferences rather than simply being
  noisy variants of the same behavior.

The clearest examples are task 1 and task 9. On task 1, `clip_w7_d4` reaches
76% while `max_pool_w3_d5` drops to 50%. On task 9, the pattern flips:
`max_pool_w3_d5` reaches 72%, `spatial16_w8_d4` reaches 60%, and
`clip_w7_d4` drops to 32%. So the builders have similar aggregate performance,
but they are not interchangeable at the task level.

Task-level interpretation:

- Task 9 has the most any-fail units (39/50), the most all-3 failures tied with task 7 (9), and the most clip-only failures (16). This is the strongest evidence that `clip_w7_d4` is specifically weak on this task.
- Task 4 has high any-fail count (34/50) and a broad spread across exact pairs and unique failures. This looks generally difficult rather than one-builder-specific.
- Task 1 has many `M+S only` failures (9) and `M only` failures (7), while `clip_w7_d4` has only 1 unique failure. This suggests the CLIP-based builder handles this task's init variation better.
- Task 7 has 9 all-3 failures but zero max-only failures. Its hard cases are shared, and `max_pool_w3_d5` does not introduce much isolated damage there.
- Task 3 is the easiest/most consistent task: only 18/50 units fail at least one builder, and the best-worst spread is just 2 successes.
